---
title: Land-Use Fragmentation and Habitat Connectivity
year: 2019
topic_tags: [land_use, biodiversity, human_impact]
---

Land-use fragmentation occurs when continuous habitat is broken into smaller,
isolated patches by roads, agriculture, or urban development. Fragmentation
reduces effective habitat area and disrupts the movement of species between
patches, which can isolate populations and reduce genetic diversity over time.

Edge effects compound the problem: fragments with a high perimeter-to-area ratio
experience altered microclimates, increased exposure to invasive species, and
greater predation pressure near patch boundaries. Species with large home ranges
or low dispersal ability are disproportionately affected by fragmentation, often
disappearing from small patches even when suitable habitat remains.

Maintaining or restoring habitat corridors between fragments is one of the most
effective mitigation strategies, allowing gene flow and recolonization after local
extinctions. Landscape-level planning that limits further fragmentation is
generally more effective than attempting to restore connectivity after the fact.
