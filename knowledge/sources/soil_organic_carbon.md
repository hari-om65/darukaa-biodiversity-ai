title: Soil Organic Carbon and Ecosystem Health
year: 2020

Soil organic carbon (SOC) is a key indicator of soil health and a major driver of
belowground biodiversity. It accumulates from decomposed plant and microbial matter
and supports the soil food web, from bacteria and fungi to earthworms and arthropods.

Higher SOC levels are strongly correlated with improved soil structure, water
retention, and nutrient cycling. Degraded soils with low organic carbon content
tend to support fewer microbial taxa and lower faunal abundance, which in turn
reduces the soil's capacity to sustain plant productivity.

Land management practices such as reduced tillage, cover cropping, and organic
amendments have been shown to rebuild SOC stocks over time. Because soil carbon
turnover is slow, restoring degraded soils to historical biodiversity baselines
can take decades, making SOC monitoring an important early-warning indicator for
land managers tracking long-term ecosystem recovery.
